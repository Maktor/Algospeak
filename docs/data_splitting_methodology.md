# Data Splitting Methodology: Group-Aware Train/Val/Test Splits

## What We Do and Why

Our dataset includes two types of related posts: real social media posts labeled as Offensive Language (class 1) or Mature Content (class 2), and synthetic algospeak versions of those same posts (class 3) generated by an LLM. For example, a post saying "I want to kill you" (class 2) might have a corresponding synthetic version saying "I want to unalive you" (class 3). Because these two posts are semantically linked — one is a deliberate coded rewrite of the other — we cannot treat them as independent data points when splitting our dataset into training, validation, and test sets. If the original post ends up in training and its algospeak version ends up in the test set, the model gets an unfair advantage: it has already seen the meaning of that post and just needs to recognize the surface-level substitution. This is a form of data leakage that would inflate our test metrics and give a false picture of how well the model generalizes to real-world unseen algospeak [5, 6].

## Why We Keep the Originals in Training

An important and non-obvious decision in our pipeline is that we keep both the original class 1/2 post and its class 3 synthetic counterpart in the training set together. An earlier version of our pipeline excluded the originals from training to be conservative, but this is the wrong approach for a specific reason that is unique to our task structure.

In standard data augmentation, an original and its augmented version belong to the same class — for example, paraphrasing a hate speech post produces another hate speech post. In our pipeline, the relationship is different: the original post ("I want to kill you") is a genuine class 2 example, and its algospeak transformation ("I want to unalive you") is a genuine class 3 example. These are not two versions of the same label — they are two distinct labeled examples from two different classes. Excluding the original from training does not prevent leakage; it simply removes a real, human-labeled class 2 example from the training set, which reduces the model's ability to learn what direct offensive content looks like and weakens the class 2 prototype. For hate speech and content moderation classifiers specifically, research consistently shows that training on the full set of original labeled examples alongside synthetic expansions produces stronger and more generalizable models than excluding originals [2, 3, 4]. The correct way to prevent leakage is not to remove originals from training, but to ensure they never cross into a different split than their paired class 3 counterpart — which is what our group-aware splitting achieves.

## How We Enforce This: Group-Aware Splitting

To satisfy both requirements — originals in training AND no leakage across splits — we use a group-aware splitting strategy implemented in `build_dataset.py`. Every original class 1/2 post and its class 3 synthetic counterpart are assigned the same group ID. When we split the dataset, we split by group rather than by individual post. This guarantees that both members of a pair always land in the same split (both in train, both in val, or both in test). All other posts that have no paired counterpart are treated as singleton groups and split normally. The result is a dataset where the model is trained on the full richness of both original and transformed posts, but is evaluated only on data it has never seen in any form — which is the correct standard for measuring generalization.
